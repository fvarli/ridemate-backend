# 6. Published routes

**Status:** accepted (Phase 10), implemented — `places` and `routes` exist and four endpoints
serve them.

## Context

After Phase 9 the product had real identity and nothing to do with it. Every screen a member
would recognise was drawing fixtures, and the client's own tests had grown a rule to keep that
honest: a presentation fixture is not a business rule.

The obvious next phase was discovery — it is the screen the design leads with. It did not
survive the audit. A match card renders eleven data points and only four could be
backend-owned; the rest name a driver, a rating, a verified badge, a trip count, an approval
rate and a Trust Score, none of which the product has. Building discovery first would have
meant **inventing people**, and putting them on the one screen whose whole purpose is to
suggest a stranger worth travelling with.

Two more things blocked it. Create Route could not honestly publish anything — it had no date
or time control at all, and departure was a client constant. And `cost_share_per_person`, on a
screen otherwise made of real inputs, had no provenance: no control, no formula, no policy.

So the phase became the narrowest thing that makes something true: **a member publishes a
journey, lists their own, and cancels one.**

## Decision

**Publish, My Routes, cancel. Discovery is not migrated.** Home, Search, Match Results and
Route Details stay wholly fixture-backed and untouched, so nothing on them gains false
authority from sitting beside real data.

**Places are immutable server-owned reference data.** Seeded, with no update path in the
source at all. The seeder is a no-op on identical data and throws when a known slug arrives
with different data, because silently moving a place moves the geography of every route
already published against it. Immutability is what lets a route hold a foreign key instead of
a snapshot: a row that cannot change cannot make a published journey lie. Coordinates and the
generated `geography` point are stored and **server-private**; the public projection is `id`
and `label`.

**PostGIS geography is stored now; the GiST index and the corridor query are deferred.** The
column costs one generated expression today and saves a backfill over live routes later. An
index, by contrast, is a write cost with no reader until a spatial query exists, so it lands
in the commit that adds one. Nothing reads `point` in Phase 10, and the docs say so rather
than implying spatial capability.

**A route's id is generated by the client.** The identifier strategy itself is ADR 0002 and is
not revisited here; what Phase 10 adds is *who mints it*. The client sends a UUIDv7 with the
publication request, which makes the identifier double as the create-idempotency mechanism: a
retry after a dropped connection carries the same id and cannot publish the journey twice.

**Idempotency becomes three alternatives, not one blanket rule.** Writing the first two
commands showed that a mandatory `Idempotency-Key` would buy a header and a storage table to
solve problems the commands had already solved by their shape. A create may rely on a stable
client-generated resource UUID; a single-target transition may rely on its target state; a
command with several meaningful outcomes still needs an explicit key, and gets one when it
first exists. `idempotency_records` is not created.

**A cancelled id is spent.** Re-publishing it is a conflict even when every field matches. A
retry is meant to be indistinguishable from the first attempt succeeding; here it succeeded
and was then deliberately undone, so returning the route would be a resurrection dressed as a
retry. No replacement is minted server-side — the client owns the id, so a genuinely new
journey brings a genuinely new one.

**Time is the server's.** A route is a plan, not an instant, because a recurring journey has
no single instant to store. A one-off carries a date and a wall clock and must be future *in
the route's own IANA zone* at publication; a weekday commute carries a wall clock and no date
and stays upcoming while it is published. The zone is server-set from one pilot configuration
value and never accepted from a client, which could otherwise move its own deadline.
`departure_state` is **derived at read time and is not a column** — the clock keeps it fresh,
and a client re-deriving it would eventually disagree about whether a journey may still be
cancelled. There is no `expired` status and no sweeper.

**Cancellation is naturally idempotent.** One target state, no body, no `expected_status`, no
key. A past one-off is refused rather than silently rewritten. A route that is not the
caller's answers exactly as one that does not exist.

**No cost field.** Not a column, a request field, a response field, a schema property, a
helper or a default. The product produces no figure any of them could honestly carry, and one
plausible amount beside real departure times is how a screen of fixtures starts looking like
server truth.

## Consequences

* The client can publish, read back and withdraw a journey against a real server. That is the
  first end-to-end product truth in the repository.
* **Discovery is now Phase 12, behind profiles.** The roadmap in `docs/architecture.md`
  records the full ordering and why it cannot be resequenced back.
* The blanket idempotency rule in `docs/api-conventions.md` is superseded. Tier 3 is not
  built, and the endpoint that needs it will build it.
* `route_occurrences` was considered and not created: a weekday route expanded into rows
  before anything queries them is storage with no reader.
* **`rule_no_pets` is a pilot blocker.** The column exists because the control exists and
  publishing while discarding it would make a working control inert. It carries an explicit
  policy-review marker: connecting it to real matching or eligibility may raise accessibility
  and non-discrimination considerations, and legal, accessibility and product review is
  required before the pilot ships. **No legal conclusion is asserted here**, and no pet policy
  is invented.
* Places being immutable is load-bearing, not incidental. Introducing place editing means
  revisiting snapshot semantics on `routes` **first**, not afterwards.
* The cursor is encrypted rather than encoded, so "opaque" stays true under inspection. What
  it encodes is free to change; a client that read one would be depending on an ordering it
  cannot see.
* Revisit points, named so they are decisions rather than drift: the GiST index and corridor
  query (Phase 12), a second city — at which point the per-row `timezone` stops being
  redundant — a third domain or a genuine cycle for `app/Modules/`, and tier-3 idempotency at
  the first multi-outcome command.
